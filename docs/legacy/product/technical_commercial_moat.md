# Commercial differentiation hypotheses

The proposed value is actionable equipment scheduling, with a record of the inputs, constraints, model, and warnings behind each recommendation. It can complement an existing commercial meter.

| Hypothesis | Evidence now | Validation needed |
|---|---|---|
| Equipment-specific advice helps staff | Windows, contiguity, priorities and resolutions | Observe use, overrides and disruption at a pilot site. |
| Local forecasting can improve prediction | MLP beats previous day at 8/10 public convenience sites; runtime ridge selects against baselines | Test unseen facility data and drift. |
| Edge forecasting remains useful during outages | Adaptive profile and RTC/NTP implementation | Physical outage, delivery, queue-loss and clock tests. |
| Tariff-aware scheduling has economic value | Formula regressions and dispatch scenarios | Reconcile actual contracts/bills and measure intervention. |
| Meter integration reduces deployment burden | Meter-reported telemetry schema | Integrate a specific driver and validate its measurement boundary. |

No exclusivity, universal accuracy, hardware certification, or payback is established. The repository is MIT-licensed; BDG2 data/adapted outputs have separate attribution/share-alike obligations. Algorithms alone are not a proprietary moat.

Durable value could come from reliable integrations, deployment tools, operator workflows, and consented facility data. Customer acquisition, support, installation, churn and pricing remain hypotheses. Do not quote competitor capabilities or prices without current primary documentation.
